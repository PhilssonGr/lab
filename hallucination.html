1 What it is

The model can write a fluent sentence that was not in the prompt and not in the source you attached. That unsupported claim is a hallucination. A claim that contradicts the source is one too.

A picture. Click a claim. The source is the only text that can support it.

Source: The warehouse closes at 18:00. Weekend hours are not listed.

Click a claim to see whether the source supports it.

2 Why it matters

Cost

An unsupported claim often needs another turn, or a person, to check it before you can use the reply.

Quality

A fluent sentence is not a source. The claim is only as good as the text that supports it.

Risk

If the product acts on the reply, an unsupported claim can become a wrong decision. Checks reduce that chance. They do not remove it.

3 How to apply · try today

Take one reply and list each factual claim. For each claim, point to a sentence in the prompt or the source. If you cannot, mark the claim unsupported.

Common mistake

Treating a confident or specific sentence as proof that the claim was in the source.

Self-check

The source says the warehouse closes at 18:00 and does not mention Sunday. The reply says it is open on Sunday. What is the Sunday claim?

Questions

What is a hallucination?
A hallucination is a reply that states something the prompt and the sources do not support.
Does fluent wording make a claim supported?
No. Support is a sentence in the prompt or the source, not the tone of the reply.
Do checks remove hallucinations?
No. They reduce the chance you act on an unsupported claim. They do not remove it.
