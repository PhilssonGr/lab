Prompting

Track · Foundations

01 What it is

Prompting is how you state the task for this turn: the instruction, the material, and the constraints. A weak prompt and a strong prompt can ask for the same outcome and still give the model different jobs.

Same task: summarize meeting notes for someone who was not there. Toggle the prompt. Highlights show what the strong version adds.

summarize this [paste notes here]
No readerNo output shapeNothing to leave outNo rule if unclear
Summarize these meeting notes for a teammate who was not there. Use three short sections: Decisions, Owners, and Open questions. Leave out small talk. If a decision is not clear in the notes, say it is unclear. Do not add decisions that are not in the notes.
  • ReaderNot named.Named: a teammate who was not there.
  • Output shapeNot set.Three short sections: decisions, owners, open questions.
  • Leave outNot set.Small talk stays out.
  • If unclearNot set.Say it is unclear. Do not invent a decision.

02 Why it matters

  • CostA vague prompt often leads to another turn to get something you can use.
  • QualityNaming the reader, the format, and what to leave out gives the reply a finish line.
  • RiskAn open prompt is easier to steer off the task, including by text inside a document you attached.

03 How to apply it

Try today

Rewrite one prompt you use so it names the reader, the output shape, and one thing to leave out.

Common mistake

Adding adjectives (“be detailed and insightful”) instead of a concrete constraint.
